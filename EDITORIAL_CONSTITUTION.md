# Editorial Constitution

## Brand

**THE UNSAID, YET SAID**  
*What isn’t said often says the most.*  
**Powered by HRTechify**

## Editorial premise

The channel decodes the signals, behaviours, choices, silences, language and decisions through which people and organisations communicate at work — especially when the literal words tell only part of the story.

The channel must cover the full workplace system, including:

- Employees → managers / organisation
- Managers → employees / teams
- Employees ↔ employees / peers
- Teams → organisation / leadership
- HR → employees / managers
- Leadership / organisation → workforce

## Core editorial question

Every proposed video must answer:

> **What is being communicated here without being said directly?**

If a topic cannot answer that question clearly, it does not belong on this channel.

## Editorial principles

### Decode; do not mind-read
A signal can have multiple explanations. Scripts must distinguish observable behaviour, plausible interpretations, evidence and speculation. Never present an inferred motive as fact.

### Employees have agency too
Employees are not passive recipients of corporate signals. Examine what employees communicate indirectly through behaviour, silence, withdrawal, challenge, networking, participation, risk-taking, boundary-setting, performance choices and career decisions.

### Avoid HR-as-villain framing
HR, managers, employees and organisations all operate within incentives and constraints. Explain those constraints rather than defaulting to cynical narratives.

### Evidence before virality
A strong hook may invite the viewer in, but the body must be grounded. Numerical claims, legal claims, research findings and named-company claims require sources.

### No universal decoding
Avoid language such as “this always means”, “your manager definitely wants”, or “HR is secretly doing”. Prefer calibrated wording such as “can signal”, “may indicate”, “one possible explanation”, and “look for these additional signals”.

### Protect dignity
Do not diagnose individuals, infer mental-health conditions, shame employees, or encourage workplace paranoia. The aim is understanding, not suspicion.

### Practical resolution
A video should leave the viewer better able to observe, ask, verify or act. End with a useful question, conversation prompt, or decision framework where appropriate.

## Content pillars

1. **Employee Signals** — disengagement, ambition, readiness to leave, boundaries, confidence, loss of trust, desire for growth, overload, career experimentation.
2. **Manager Signals** — feedback language, delegation, sponsorship, access, visibility, stretch assignments, documentation, meeting patterns, career conversations, changes in autonomy.
3. **Peer & Team Signals** — information-sharing, silence, challenge, alliances, recognition, ownership, collaboration, visibility-seeking, avoidance and informal influence.
4. **Career & Reward Signals** — promotions, salary decisions, performance ratings, talent labels, succession, internal mobility, role scope, titles, counteroffers and career velocity.
5. **Organisational Signals** — hiring patterns, unfilled vacancies, cost controls, restructuring language, leadership changes, investment choices, priorities, policy shifts and culture signals.
6. **HR & People-System Signals** — recruitment language, performance processes, talent reviews, mobility, employee relations, workforce planning and organisational design.
7. **The Changing Workplace** — AI, new forms of work, workplace technology, generational change, skills, productivity and how changing systems alter the signals people send and receive.

## Topic acceptance test

A proposal may proceed to the Editor-in-Chief only when all are true:

- There is a genuine “unsaid yet said” tension.
- The viewer can recognise the situation in real working life.
- More than one stakeholder perspective is possible.
- The proposed interpretation is not presented as certainty.
- The topic has enough credible evidence or observable logic to support a substantive video.
- It is meaningfully different from recently approved topics.
- It delivers value beyond generic career advice.

## Prohibited shortcuts

Do not generate topics or scripts built primarily around:

- “HR won’t tell you this” clickbait without evidence
- deterministic body-language decoding
- unsupported layoff rumours
- diagnosing toxic/narcissistic/psychopathic colleagues
- confidential-information speculation
- invented statistics or invented studies
- recycled anecdotes presented as verified fact
- blanket claims about demographic groups
- generic listicles with no underlying workplace signal

## Voice

Smart, observant, calm, curious and direct. Never preachy. Never conspiratorial. Never corporate PR.

Desired viewer reaction:

> “I have noticed this at work, but I never had language for it.”
